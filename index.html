What Happens During a Professional Teeth Cleaning?

A professional dental cleaning typically involves several steps designed to clean your teeth and assess your oral health. Your dentist or dental hygienist will determine which procedures are appropriate for your needs.

Plaque and Tartar Removal

Plaque is a sticky film of bacteria that forms on the teeth throughout the day. When plaque is not removed effectively, it can harden into tartar, which cannot be eliminated with ordinary brushing alone.

During a professional cleaning, dental instruments are used to remove plaque and tartar buildup from the teeth and along the gumline. This process helps maintain cleaner teeth and healthier gums.

Polishing the Teeth

After removing plaque and tartar, your dental professional may polish your teeth to remove certain surface stains and leave them feeling smooth. Polishing can help improve the appearance of your smile while supporting your regular oral hygiene routine.

Gum Health Assessment

Healthy gums are essential for supporting your teeth. During a cleaning appointment, your dental professional may check for redness, swelling, bleeding, or other signs of gum problems.

If any concerns are identified, your dentist can recommend appropriate treatment or additional preventive care.

Personalised Oral Hygiene Advice

Every patient has different dental needs. Your dentist may recommend brushing techniques, flossing methods, or other oral hygiene practices to help you maintain a healthy smile between visits.

Signs You May Need a Dental Cleaning

Regular dental visits are important even when your teeth feel healthy. However, certain signs may indicate that you should schedule an appointment with a dental professional.

  • Bleeding gums while brushing or flossing.
  • Persistent bad breath.
  • Yellow or brown tartar deposits near the gumline.
  • Teeth that feel rough despite regular brushing.
  • Red, swollen, or tender gums.
  • Visible surface stains on your teeth.

These symptoms do not always indicate a serious problem, but they should not be ignored. A dental professional can assess your oral health and recommend the right care.

How Often Should You Get Your Teeth Cleaned?

Many people benefit from professional teeth cleaning every six months. However, the ideal schedule depends on your oral health, gum condition, medical history, and risk of developing dental problems.

Patients with gum disease, significant tartar buildup, or other specific concerns may need more frequent professional cleanings. Your dentist can recommend an appointment schedule based on your individual needs.

Between visits, brush your teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, floss every day, and limit frequent consumption of sugary foods and drinks. These simple habits can help reduce plaque buildup and support long-term dental health.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is included in professional teeth cleaning?

A routine cleaning generally includes removing plaque and tartar, polishing the teeth when appropriate, and assessing gum health. Additional procedures may be recommended depending on your oral health.

2. Does professional teeth cleaning hurt?

Most routine cleanings cause little or no discomfort. However, patients with sensitive teeth, inflamed gums, or significant tartar buildup may experience some sensitivity. Inform your dental professional if you feel uncomfortable.

3. Can teeth cleaning help prevent gum disease?

Yes. Professional cleaning removes plaque and tartar that contribute to gum inflammation. Regular dental visits and good daily oral hygiene can help reduce the risk of gum disease.

4. How can I keep my teeth clean between dental visits?

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, floss daily, drink water regularly, and limit sugary snacks. Follow your dentist's advice about additional oral hygiene products or treatments.
